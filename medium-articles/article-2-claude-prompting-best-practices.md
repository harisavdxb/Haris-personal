# 10 Claude Prompting Best Practices That Will Change How You Work

## Why Most People Use Claude Wrong

You open Claude. You type a question. You get an answer. You move on.

That's fine. But it's like buying a Ferrari and never leaving second gear.

Claude is architecturally different from other AI models. It was built to handle nuance, follow complex multi-part instructions, and reason through hard problems — but only if you engage with it properly.

Here are the 10 practices that separate average users from power users.

---

## Best Practice #1: Assign a Role First

Claude responds to identity. Before any complex task, give it a professional role.

**Weak prompt:**
> "Review my business plan."

**Strong prompt:**
> "You are a seasoned venture capitalist who has reviewed over 500 early-stage startups. Review this business plan and give me your honest, critical assessment — focus on market size assumptions and competitive moat."

The output shifts from generic to genuinely expert.

---

## Best Practice #2: Give It the Full Context

Claude has a 200,000 token context window. Use it.

Don't summarize your document — paste the whole thing. Don't describe your code — share the full file. The more real context Claude has, the less it has to guess.

> **Rule of thumb:** If you're paraphrasing information Claude could just read directly, you're limiting your results.

---

## Best Practice #3: Tell It the Output Format

If you want a bullet list, say so. If you want a table, specify it. If you want a 200-word paragraph followed by three key takeaways, describe that structure explicitly.

**Example:**
> "Give me the response as:
> 1. A one-sentence executive summary
> 2. Three supporting arguments (2–3 sentences each)
> 3. One counterargument and how to address it"

Claude will follow that structure precisely.

---

## Best Practice #4: Use the "Think Step by Step" Trigger

For reasoning-heavy tasks — math, logic, strategy, diagnosis — explicitly ask Claude to think before answering.

> "Think through this step by step before giving me your final answer."

This activates more thorough reasoning and dramatically reduces errors on complex problems.

---

## Best Practice #5: Set the Audience

Claude calibrates its language and depth when you specify who you're writing for.

- "Explain this to a 12-year-old."
- "Write this for a PhD-level audience."
- "Make this accessible to someone with no tech background."

Specifying audience = better-calibrated output every single time.

---

## Best Practice #6: Use Iterative Refinement, Not One-Shot Prompting

Most people expect magic on the first try. Real power users iterate.

Start broad, then refine:

1. First pass: "Draft an email to my team about the project delay."
2. Second pass: "Good. Now make the tone warmer and cut it by 30%."
3. Third pass: "Remove the third paragraph and add a clear call-to-action at the end."

Treat Claude like a collaborative editor, not a vending machine.

---

## Best Practice #7: Ask It to Challenge You

Most AI defaults to agreement. Claude can be instructed to disagree.

> "I'm going to share my strategy. After I do, give me the strongest possible argument AGAINST it. Don't hold back."

This is one of the most underused and most valuable use cases for Claude.

---

## Best Practice #8: Specify Length and Depth

Vague prompts produce vague-length responses. Control it.

- "In exactly 3 sentences..."
- "Give me a comprehensive 1,000-word analysis..."
- "Keep this under 100 words."

Claude respects length constraints well. Use them.

---

## Best Practice #9: Chain Complex Tasks

Break large tasks into a sequence of smaller prompts.

Instead of: "Write a full marketing strategy for my product."

Try:
1. "First, identify the top 3 customer personas for [product]."
2. "Now, for each persona, what is their primary pain point that [product] solves?"
3. "Now write one positioning statement per persona."
4. "Now build a 90-day content calendar targeting persona #1."

Chaining produces higher-quality output at every step.

---

## Best Practice #10: Ask for Sources and Uncertainty

Claude is trained to acknowledge what it doesn't know. Take advantage of that.

End research prompts with:
> "Flag any areas where you're uncertain, and tell me what I'd need to verify independently."

This makes Claude a safer, more reliable research tool — especially for professional or high-stakes work.
